## The peoples of Eyrland

### Humans
Most of Eyrland. River-trade folk, practical, proud of their cities, suspicious of other cities. Midriver humans love magic trinkets, festivals and a good wager.

### Dwarves
Under the Kettlehorns. Formal, clan-based, oath-bound, superb smiths and engineers. **They refuse to use glimmer** ("we don't burn what we didn't make"), and humans think that's stubbornness. Some dwarves live in Emberford (the Iron Kettle). Dwarves never speak of the Gate War except to say "the Bear held."

### Elves
In the Westwood. Long-lived, slow, polite, sad. Not tricksy, not cruel. They keep promises absolutely and make few. Elves who leave the forest are rare and usually exiles or wanderers.

### Gnomes
In the Hobbland. Bright, inventive, sociable, overconfident. They ruled the hill country by lottery for 300 years and think everyone else is mad for not doing so.

### Halflings and others
A few halfling barge families on the Drift, a scattering of half-elves in the Fringe and Tollhaven. No dragonborn, tieflings or anything demon-adjacent in this world. **Tieflings don't exist here.**

### Beastfolk in Eyrland (at campaign start)
Rare. Most Eyrlanders have *never seen one*. They're known from folk tales ("the talking-beast country under the water") and from the occasional odd traveler. A tabaxi trader shows up in Saltreach once every few years and is treated like a celebrity. Increasing sightings are the visible clock (see SEAL track).

## Faith

Classic and light. No hells, no devils, no demon lords. Evil comes from mortal choices, ancient things and corruption, not an infernal plane.

| Faith | Domain | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Bright Lady** (aka the Lamp-Mother, Lady of the Ford) | Light, hearth, rivers, mercy | Everywhere; Temple Isle in Emberford | Main faith of the Midriver. Lamps are her sign. Priests wear blue and carry a small lamp. |
| **The Stonefather** | Mountains, craft, oaths | Dwarves | Dwarven faith. Oaths sworn on stone are sacred. |
| **The Green Mother** | Growth, beasts, seasons | Farmers, druids, Aranhollow (as "the Green") | Old faith; druids and rangers. Paladins of the Ancients swear by "the green and the light." |
| **The Tide King** | Sea, storms, luck | Saltreach | Sailors' god; offerings of salt and coin. |
| **The Quiet** (DM: a front) | Peace, rest, grief | Small, new, growing in Emberford | A gentle "consolation circle" for the grieving. **DM: the public face of the Calm (FAC-03).** |

**DM note (sealed):** The Bright Lady's worship began as remembrance of **Wynne Fenn-wyck**, the first Hearkener, "the lady with the lamp at the ford." Her lamp was the light she sang by. Temple iconography shows a woman at a river with a stag beside her (seed: the stag is in every old temple mural, and nobody asks why).

## Everyday life in the Midriver
- **Money:** all coin counts as gold (house rule). Locals still say "a copper's worth" and "silver tongue," and mean small and smooth.
- **Food:** river fish, eel pie, black bread, cheese, apples and cider from the Hobbland, hot spiced wine at festivals, honey-cakes on Lantern Night.
- **Drink:** ale (cheap), cider (cheaper), Hobbland brandy (dangerous), dwarven stout (expensive), elven wine (almost never seen).
- **Clothing:** practical wool and linen, bright scarves, lamp-charm buttons. Crownhill wears silk and glimmer-thread.
- **Literacy:** most guild folk read; Fringe folk mostly don't. Ash reads well enough (Zeke taught him and won't let him forget it).
- **Orphans:** common after the river fever years. Lamp House on Temple Isle takes them in. Fringe families take each other's kids in.

## Common knowledge (player-safe)
- Nobody in Eyrland has seen a talking beast except in stories, until lately.
- The Bright Lady's temple sits on Temple Isle. Most people tap a lamp for her blessing.
- A new consolation circle called **the Quiet** meets in Old Ford for people who've lost someone. It's said to really help.
- Dwarves won't touch glimmer. Elves don't sell it. Gnomes complain it's "imprecise."
