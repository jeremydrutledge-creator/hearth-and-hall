## The shape of Eyrland

Eyrland is one great river basin. Almost everything that matters lies along the **Tamber River** and its tributaries. Picture a tilted bowl: mountains on the north rim, forest on the west, rolling hill country on the east, and the sea at the bottom, in the south.

### Key features

| Feature | Description | Pronunciation / alt spellings |
|---|---|---|
| **Tamber River** | The great river. Wide, brown-green, slow in the lowlands, fast and cold in the north. Barge traffic the whole way from Copperhill to the sea. | TAM-ber (Tambor, Tamberr) |
| **Kettlehorn Mountains** | Grey peaks with steaming hot springs ("kettles") in high valleys. Dwarven country. | (Kettle Horns, Kettlehorns) |
| **Bearback Pass** | The high pass through the Kettlehorns. A long humped ridge shaped like a sleeping bear. | (Bear Back) |
| **Lake Corrie** | Small, deep, cold lake below the foothills. Trout, fog, an old monastery. | KOR-ee (Lake Cory, Corey) |
| **Brindle River** | Western tributary out of the elven forest. Clear, quick, cold. Elves allow only small boats. | (Brindel) |
| **Fordwater** | The arm of the Tamber that splits around Emberford's islands. The famous shallow ford is here. | (Ford Water) |
| **Hobb River** | Eastern tributary from the hill country. Muddy, twisty, full of mills. | (Hob) |
| **The Glasswater** | The great lake. A day and a half to cross by sail. Tollhaven and Driftmarket are here. It's called the Glasswater because it's usually smooth, which no one thinks twice about. | (Glass Water) |
| **Greymarsh** | Wide reed marsh south of the Glasswater. Mist, herons, ruins. | (Gray Marsh) |
| **Tamber Delta** | Braided channels, islands, smugglers. | |
| **The Sundersea** | The southern ocean. Bounded: sailors know its shape, with coasts to east and west and the far shore of the Salt Isles. Not endless. | (Sunder Sea) |
| **The Glass** | A calm patch of sea a day's sail out. Ships avoid it: no wind, no waves, no fish. | |
| **Ommu's Rest** | Island in the Glass, charted "uninhabited, avoid." Strange domed hills. | OH-moo |

## Regions

1. **The North (Kettlehorn country):** dwarves, mines, mountain passes, hot springs, goats, mercenaries at Wolfsgate. Cold.
2. **The Westwood (Aranhollow):** elven forest, enormous trees, few roads. Humans enter by invitation.
3. **The Midriver (Emberford country):** the heart of trade and magic. Farms, orchards, mills, the great city.
4. **The Hobbland (east hills):** gnome towns, sheep, cider, small mills, clever toys.
5. **The Lakeland (Glasswater):** fishing, merchant money, barge clans.
6. **The Lowriver (Greymarsh to Highmarch):** the old kingdom heartland. Castles, the capital, the marsh and its ruins.
7. **The Coast (Delta and Saltreach):** salt, sailors, smugglers, the sea.

## Travel

### By river (the normal way)
| Leg | Downstream | Upstream | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copperhill → Emberford | 2 days | 4 days | Rapids at the Kettle Steps; barges portage |
| Emberford → Tollhaven (Glasswater) | 1.5 days | 3 days | Busiest stretch on the river |
| Tollhaven → Highmarch | 3 days | 6 days | Passes the Greymarsh |
| Highmarch → Saltreach | 2 days | 4 days | The delta; slow, many channels |
| Emberford → Hobb's Ferry | half a day | 1 day | |
| Emberford → Bramblecog (Hobb River) | 2 days | 3 days | Small boats only past the mills |
| Emberford → Aranhollow edge (Brindle) | — | 3 days | Elves meet boats at the Leaf Weir |

**Barge passage:** 2 gp/day per person for deck space and simple meals; 5 gp/day for a cabin. Working passage (load, pole, stand watch) is free and very common for young men like Ash. A **fast packet boat** (sail and oars) halves downstream time at 10 gp/day.

### By road
- **The King's Road** follows the river's east bank from Highmarch to Copperhill. Patrolled near cities, bandits between.
- **The Mill Road** runs east to the Hobbland.
- **The Pass Road** climbs from Copperhill to Wolfsgate and Bearback Pass to Dun Morrach. Closed by snow in deep winter.
- Walking pace: 24 miles/day. Horse at travel pace: 30–40 miles/day.

### By sea
Saltreach to the Salt Isles: 4 days. Saltreach to the Glass: 1 day (if anyone will take you).

## Weather and calendar

- **Year of the Ford (YF).** Campaign opens in **early spring, YF 1104**.
- Four seasons with real winters in the north. River floods in late spring ("the Highwater").
- **Months (common names):** Thawing, Seedtide, Highwater, Blossom, Midsummer, Haymonth, Harvest, Leaffall, Frostcome, Deepwinter, Longnight, Lantern. Each has 30 days. **Lantern** is the year-end month of festivals and lights.
- **Key days:** Midsummer's Eve (Ash's birthday; the Turn-of-Light festival), the Ford Festival (the 1st of Leaffall, city founding), Highwater Fair (Emberford, mid-Highwater), Lantern Night (the last night of the year, every lamp in Emberford lit).

## Common knowledge (player-safe)
- The Tamber River is the road of Eyrland. If you want to go somewhere, you take a barge.
- The dwarves live under the Kettlehorns and are stiff about strangers. The elves live in the Westwood and are stiffer.
- Gnomes in the Hobbland make the best clockwork and the worst cider puns.
- Tollhaven is where money lives. Highmarch is where the king lives. Saltreach is where the sea starts and honest men stop.
- Sailors steer around a dead-calm stretch of sea called the Glass. Nobody knows why it's calm. Fishermen say "don't wish on still water."
- Wolfsgate is a mercenary town. You can hire anything there, including trouble.
- Greymarsh has old ruins full of ghosts. Children dare each other to row out to them.

## DM notes (sealed)
- **Every major city sits on a Wellgate.** Emberford (the Ford Pool and the Hall of Two Rivers beneath), Tollhaven (a flooded counting-house vault), Highmarch (the castle well), Saltreach (the old lighthouse cistern), Dun Morrach (the sealed Deep Spring, dwarven-guarded), Aranhollow (the Queen's Mirror pool). Also minor gates: Lake Corrie's monastery well, a millpond in Bramblecog, a drowned chapel in Old Varrow.
- **"The Glasswater" is a near-gate.** The whole lake goes still at the turn of light. It's called that because the seal runs close under it.
