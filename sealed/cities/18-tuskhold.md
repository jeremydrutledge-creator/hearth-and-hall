# Tuskhold (Mirrowen): Side-Activity Menu (SEALED — DM ONLY)

> **Tuskhold** (aka Tusk Hold, Tuskhould). The elephant-folk stone city on Mirrowen's great plain. Mirrors Highmarch.
> **Government:** **the Rememberer**, the eldest matriarch and keeper of memory. **Grandmother Umbari** (NPC-021; aka Ombari, Umbaree) is Rememberer at campaign start, besieged by Brannoc (`world/05`). By the end of Ch16, **Nkemba** (NPC-183) becomes Rememberer (`chapters/act3/ch16.md`). **Law:** the **Wallguard** and the **Judges of the Long Stone** (elephant-folk judges whose rulings are carved, never spoken twice).
> **When visited:** Ch14 (the siege lines; side window: memory-stones, wall-watch with Nkemba, feeding the city), Ch16 (Tuskhold's battle and Umbari's funeral; side window: the Memory Contest, the Long-Stone Puzzle, the Horned Wrestle, Waking the Plain, Ama's Sky, Bart's Funeral). **Tier: T3.**
> **The chapter wins.** Both chapters' windows are full. This file adds shops, the city's look and customs, odd jobs for quiet days, and what the city becomes.

## 1. The city at a glance

- **Look:** a city of great grey stone blocks on a rise in a sea of grass; every wall carved, floor to roof, with **long-stones** (memory carvings: whole family histories in pictures and script); a central **Remembering** (a stone amphitheater of carved standing slabs); wide ramps instead of stairs; fountains for drinking with a trunk.
- **Sound:** low rumbles of elephant-folk speech you feel in your chest; the scratch of carving chisels day and night; during the siege, Horned drums outside.
- **Feel:** patient, wise, and remembering everything. They never forget a kindness or a debt, and they are very slow to anger and very hard to stop once angry. They're Mirrowen's historians and judges.
- **Custom:** a guest is asked one question at the gate: *"What do you want remembered?"* Whatever the answer, it's carved, small, on the guest-stone by the gate. (Ask the player. Write it down. Ch16's funeral and Ch20's gathering can find it.)

## 2. Shops and services

Elephant-folk don't haggle: they remember what you paid last time and charge the same. **The second visit** to any shop costs exactly what the first did, whatever the war has done to prices. (A kindness, and a joke.)

### 2.1 Oyelu's (the Stonewrights' Ramp)
**Keeper: Oyelu the Stonewright** (NPC-355; aka Oyello, Oy-YAY-loo), an old elephant-folk carver and smith who makes armor out of stone-hard hide-plates and carves long-stones for families who can't.
**Interior (painter):** a cavern-workshop under a ramp, stone dust in shafts of light, half-carved slabs leaning like books, great hide-and-stone armor on frames, chisels the size of swords.

### 2.2 The Rememberers' Hall
Not a shop. **Memory-stones** (Ch14): sit with a Rememberer and listen to one long-stone (an hour; History DC 15). **Research here is at advantage** for any question about Mirrowen's past, the Gate War, the Firstbeasts or Ursk.

### 2.3 The Fountain Court (food and rest)
Tuskhold feeds guests: flatbread, fruit, a thick bean stew. Free, always, even during the siege (Ch14 "Feed the city" is the party feeding *them* back).

## 3. Contests

- **Ch16's funeral games** (the Memory Contest, the Long-Stone Puzzle, the Horned Wrestle) are the city's great contests. Run them as written there.
- **The Pushing Stone** (any calm day, outside Ch16): two contestants push a carved stone from opposite sides along a groove. **Strength (Athletics)** contest best of three against an elephant-folk pusher (+10). Gideon's event. **Win:** the pusher bows, and carves your name in the groove's end (the oldest names are four hundred years old).
- **Riddles of the Rememberers:** an elder asks one riddle a day at the Fountain Court. Answer it and she tells you one true thing about Mirrowen's past. **Intelligence DC 17.** Use real riddles if the player likes.

### Skill contests (`00-index.md` §11; CHG-21)
- **The Weight-and-Balance** (Gauntlet, T3): carry a water jar across the balance-logs of the old wall (**Dex save** ⬇ and **Con save** ⏱ stations, DC 16). The elephant-folk children are better at it than anyone. **No fee; prize: a memory-stone with your name on it**, and food for the city's refugees in the winner's name.
- **Trunk-Throw** (knife-throwing, T3): elephant-folk swing the targets with their trunks (**moving, AC 16–24**). **Prize 200 gp** (in food for the besieged city if the party agrees, which earns a step of attitude with the Rememberer).

## 4. Fights

**Ring formats (`00-index.md` §5.1, CHG-19):** the Horned Wrestle (Ch16) is **Ring-Out** in a sand circle; the elephant-folk also play **Touches** with reed-wands for children's festivals and will let an outsider in. No magic.

**No ring.** Tuskhold's only ring is the war on the walls (Ch14, Ch16). Sparring happens on the wall-walk with the **Wallguard**, who'll spar with anyone who stands a watch with them (no purse; respect). Ch16's **Horned Wrestle** (Brannoc +13) is the one honor bout.

---

## 5. Betting

Elephant-folk think betting is a kind of forgetting ("You'll remember winning, and forget the man who lost"). The **Wallguard** bet anyway, quietly, in carved pebbles, on how many Horned drums they'll hear tonight.

## 7. Things worth stealing

**Nothing.** Theft in Tuskhold is carved on the thief's family's long-stone forever. No elephant-folk family has had that carving in two hundred years. **Fence:** none in Tuskhold, ever.

## 8. Tuskhold by act

| When | What it's like |
|---|---|
| **Ch14** | Under siege. Starving, patient, unbroken. Umbari is dying and keeps not doing it, "waiting for a boy." |

| **After Ch16** | The plain is waking. The city is rebuilding its outer wall, and every new block has a long-stone carving on it of the siege, including one of three Tall Folk brothers, a druid, a small hare and a very rude owl. |
