# Emberford: Magic Shops and Neighborhood Merchants (SEALED — DM ONLY)

> Emberford's seven magic shops (`world/06`) and the ordinary merchants the Fringe, the Commons and Market Steps live on. Every keeper has an NPC ID from `truth/00`. Prices are 2024 book prices (`00-index.md` §2): Common 100 · Uncommon 400 · Rare 4,000 · Very Rare 40,000; consumables half; weapons and armor add the base item.
> **Stock by act.** Each shop's inventory is split into what's on the shelves from the start (Act I), what turns up in Act II (L7–13) and what's available in Act III (L14–20). Higher-rarity stock is *on request*: the keeper sends for it or makes it, with a lead time. This keeps the shelves in line with the chapter treasure budgets in `rules/02` and the chapter files.
> **Glimmer note.** Items marked **(g)** have a glimmer core and **die if glimmer dies** (Endings 1 and 2; `campaign/05` §4). Unmarked items are true magic and survive every ending. The keepers know which is which, and Hakon Stonebrow will tell you at length.

## 1. The Patient Owl

**Keeper:** **Master Ambrose Webb** (NPC-046; aka Ambros Web, Ambrose Weeb). Ancient, bat-eared, glacially slow. No ledger edges yet; he has no special knowledge of anyone, and he likes Zeke (play it as warm silence).
**Where:** Glimmerway, at the dead end of Inkpot Lane, under the Lyceum's crooked tower. Open "when the owl is awake": mid-morning to dusk, never on Tenth-days (too loud).

**Personality.** He won't sell to the rude or the rushed. He answers questions a minute after you've asked them. He rewards patience with discounts and with *secrets*. He has a stuffed owl over the door that he talks to, and the owl is, he insists, the real proprietor.
**Voice:** Long pauses. Finishes other people's sentences wrong. *"…Yes. No. The second one. You want the second one. Sit. The kettle will think about boiling presently."*

**Interior (for the painter).** A tall, narrow shop three stories high with no floors between the second and third, just a gallery and a rolling ladder. Books in stacks like stalagmites. Scroll-cases in honeycomb racks, every cubby labeled in faded brown ink. One candle-lamp of *real wax* (he won't have glimmer near the paper). The stuffed owl over the door, its glass eyes very slightly mismatched. A brass kettle on a spirit stove that is always nearly boiling. Dust in the light from a round window. A worn armchair for customers, deliberately placed so you can't see the counter from it ("so you stop *looking* at me").

**Haggle:** DC 15. **Patience lever:** if the buyer sits in the armchair for an hour without hurrying him (no check if the player says so and means it; a restless character makes a **Wisdom DC 12** check), he takes 10% off and tells one true thing. Rushing him or snapping at him: no sale this visit.
**The one true thing (roll d6 or pick):** 1, the Lyceum's crooked tower has a room nobody's opened in sixty years. 2, "Glimmer sings, you know. Badly." 3, an old charter in the Guild records names "the ford-watch." 4, a spell to unweave enchantments works better with a tuning fork in the off hand (it doesn't; he's wrong, and Zeke can tell him so, and he's delighted). 5, the Calm's chapel bought every book on grief he had (SEC-04 color; Act II only). 6, "Your brother reads too fast. Tell him from me." Use only what fits the chapter; none of it is a required seed.

## 2. Brightwell & Daughters

**Keepers:** **Madame Ottoline Brightwell** (NPC-047; aka Otteline, Brightwel) and her three daughters: **Clementine** (NPC-300; the eldest, the one who handles money, cool as a coin), **Rosamund** (NPC-301; the fashion eye, warm once you're a customer) and **Violet** (NPC-302; the youngest, bored, kind to Fringe folk because it annoys her mother). No ledger edges; she's a Crownhill gossip line. If the Crownhill heist from Ch5 (Osric's signet ring) went through here, she remembers the Fenwick face and frowns.
**Where:** Crownhill, the corner of Lantern Walk and the Provost's Row. Glass front, gilt letters, a doorman (a big polite half-orc in a lilac coat named **Barnabus**; T3).

**Personality.** Glossy, glittering, expensive, glimmer-fashion. Snobbish to Fringe folk until you have money, and then very, very welcoming.
**Voice (Ottoline):** a purr that drops a register when she smells coin. *"Oh, how* delightful. *Violet, darling, fetch the… no, the* other *tray."*

**Entry rule.** Anyone in fine clothes, or anyone visibly carrying 50+ gp, is greeted by Ottoline or Clementine. Anyone else gets Violet, at the side counter, and **disadvantage on the haggle** (Violet can't discount; she has to ask). Ash owns fine clothes (15 gp). **Haggle DC 16.** **Snob lever:** name a Crownhill client or a guildmaster who'd "approve" (**Deception or Persuasion DC 14**): advantage on the haggle. Mentioning the Fringe, at any point: disadvantage.

**Interior (for the painter).** White marble floor with a gold inlaid lamp-sigil. Glass cases on brass legs; inside them, glimmer jewelry glowing in soft colors like sweets. Three tall mirrors that show you slightly better-looking (a glimmer charm in each). Velvet ottomans in rose and lilac. A spiral staircase to the fitting rooms, with a lamp at each turn. The air smells of violet perfume. A tray of tiny iced cakes for clients, which Violet gives to Fringe children through the back door.

## 3. Odd Lot

**Keeper:** **Jory Tolliver** (NPC-045; aka Jorrie, Tolliver, Jorey). Sly half-elf pawnbroker and fence, gap-toothed grin. Ash's main fence. No ledger edge; he's friendly to anyone with something to sell. Don't confuse him with the Tolley brothers (NPC-044).
**Where:** Market Steps, third terrace down, wedged between a fishmonger and a birdseller. Open dawn to whenever Jory falls asleep.

**Personality.** Chaos. Junk to the rafters. Half the magic has a quirk. Haggling is the national sport, and the price on the tag is a joke he's telling you. He doesn't ask where things came from. **His soft spot:** Fringe keepsakes. He'll take them, but once he knows something's from the Fringe, he keeps it on the "back shelf" for the owner to buy back at cost.
**Voice:** fast, delighted, always mid-deal. *"Ash! My favorite liar. What've you got, what d'you need, and how much are you going to lie to me about either?"*

**Interior (for the painter).** A cave of things. Birdcages hanging from the ceiling with no birds, one with a clock in it. A suit of rusty armor wearing a lady's hat. A wall of keys. Jars of buttons, teeth, marbles, glass eyes. Rugs on rugs. A brass counter buried under ledgers, a pawn ticket spike, and a stuffed pike with a pipe in its mouth. The light is three mismatched glimmer lamps, one flickering. A "back shelf" behind a curtain where the hot goods and the keepsakes live. Jory sits on a high stool like a heron.

**Haggle:** DC 12. **Counter-offer lever:** if Ash makes a counter-offer that's funny or outrageous (the player's actual line), roll at advantage. **Jory's rule:** he always comes down at least once ("for love").

### How the shelves work
Odd Lot's magic is **secondhand and quirky**. An item without a quirk sells at book (Jory knows the difference).
**Each visit, roll 1d4 + 2 times on the shelf table** (or pick). Restock every Tenth-day.

## 4. Mother Brine's

**Keeper:** **Mother Brine** (NPC-043; real name Bethany Rourke; aka Beth, "Brine," Mother Bryne). Retired sailor, wooden leg, foghorn voice. Tough love. Can treat Martha Pratt (EVT-55). No ledger edges; she has no special knowledge of the party beyond what the Wharves know.
**Where:** the Wharves, on the Low Wharf next to the Wharfmaster's Office, in the hull of an upturned barge with a door cut in its side. Open at dawn for the fishing crews and again at dusk.

**Personality.** Gruff, warm, salty, teases everyone. Calls every man "pup" and every woman "duck." Her potions work, and she'll tell you exactly what will happen if you drink three at once.
**Voice:** foghorn. *"Pup! You're bleeding on my floor. That's a copper for the mop. Sit. Drink this. Don't make the face. Make the face, see what happens."*

**Interior (for the painter).** The inside of an upturned barge: ribs overhead like a whale's belly, tarred and hung with drying kelp, rope and lanterns. Shelves made of old oars. Bottles in every color, stoppered with cork and wax, each labeled in her big square hand ("FOR BLEEDING," "FOR STUPIDITY"). A copper still bubbling in the corner. A ship's wheel nailed to the wall as decoration, a parrot cage with no parrot ("He left me. Joined the navy."). A scrubbed table with a bowl of salt for luck. Her wooden leg propped on a crate, carved with mermaids.

**Haggle:** DC 14. **Sea-story lever:** tell her a good tale (**Charisma (Performance or Deception) DC 13**; she knows when you're lying and likes it anyway): −10% for the visit. Regulars (three visits or more) get **"the Brine price": −10% on Potions of Healing, always.**

## 5. The Iron Kettle

**Keeper:** **Hakon Stonebrow** (NPC-049; aka Haakon, Hakkon). Dwarf smith in exile by choice. Hates glimmer. Keeps the Oath of the Quiet Stone (SEC-11); after Ch10 he'll break it for Gideon. No ledger edges yet. Gideon's favorite shop.
**Where:** the Commons edge, where the forges meet Glimmerway, down six stone steps into a half-cellar. A kettle-shaped iron sign. Open from first light; shut "when the iron says."

**Personality.** Hot, loud, honest. Pure dwarven forge-work, no glimmer, no exceptions. Higher prices, top quality, lifetime guarantee ("my lifetime, not yours"). Treats Gideon like a nephew and Ash like a suspect.
