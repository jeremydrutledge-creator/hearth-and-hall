## 1. ★ Must-happen beats (with routes)

### ★1 · The Ford Pool
Ash learns where Moss goes every morning: a perfectly still pool deep in the Fordwood, ringed with mossy standing stones carved in an alphabet nobody can read (except Zeke, it turns out).
- **A. Moss invites them**, because the call is getting louder and he can't hide it any more. *"Come with me tomorrow, lad. Before light. Bring your brothers. Bring that knife-happy hare."*
- **B. Ash follows Moss out the West Gate at dawn** (Stealth vs. Moss's passive Perception 14). **Moss lets him succeed.** Run **Into the Fordwood** (§4). At the pool, without turning round: "You'd better come out, then. Mind the roots."
- **C. Thistle leads them:** *"The gate. The old one. Mother Burdock said the Stag's people keep it."* Moss is furious, then resigned.
- **D. The party avoids the Fordwood entirely:** see ★2 Route C. The Fordwood comes to them.

### ★2 · Something comes through
At the turn of light (sunset), the pool shivers. Greywilt crawls up the stones. **Hushed** creatures come up out of the water: Hushed wolves, and a Hushed bear-folk soldier of Brannoc's Hollowmere patrol who blundered into the gate.
- **A. At sunset, with the party at the pool** (Fight 2 as written, §5).
- **B. If the party isn't at the pool**, the Hushed come out of the Fordwood onto the **Westwood Road** at dusk and attack an orchard cart. Ash hears the gate *tear*, a sound like cloth ripping underwater. Fight 2's Phase 1 on the road; Phases 2–3 at the pool, which Moss now *has* to take them to, at a run (the pool's still tearing; arrive at the start of Phase 2).
- **C. If the party avoids the Fordwood altogether**, a Hushed wolf turns up in the Bend at the bottom of Willow Lane at dusk, grey and dripping and silent among the children's eel traps. Then Moss *has* to take them to the pool. (Run the Bend wolf as a one-creature skirmish; Fight 2 then begins at the pool the next sunset, with the tear already at stage 1.)

### ★3 · Ash's hum steadies the gate
Mid-fight, the gate begins to tear wider (Phase 2→3). Moss is straining to hold it. Ash, without knowing why, hums his tune, and the pool *listens*. The tear slows. It doesn't close (closing takes Voice; `campaign/00` §2), but it **holds**.
- **A. Ash chooses to hum.** Prompt with sensation, never instruction: *"The tune is in your throat. The water is waiting."*
- **B. Ash is knocked unconscious or prone near the water:** the hum comes out of him on its own, half-dreaming. (He makes the first Hold the Gate check at disadvantage, but he makes it.)
- **C. Thistle shouts:** *"Sing! You're the hearer. Sing!"* And the moment is his to take.
- **D. Ash refuses, or is elsewhere entirely:** Moss holds it by hand, alone, and it costs him (see §5 Fail-forward). The beat still lands, smaller and later: that night, Ash hums at the Lane Well without meaning to, and in the Fordwood, Moss feels the pool steady, and weeps. Ash hears Lark say "That was you. I felt it."

### ★4 · Zeke reads the stones
After the fight, Zeke finds he can read the carvings, slowly, like a half-remembered language. One stone says: *"Here the Ford-Watcher and the Hart sang the world in two."* Moss goes white. *"Read it again, Ezekiel."* (Moss never calls him Ezekiel.)
- **A. Zeke studies the stones** (he will; try stopping him).
- **B. If Zeke wasn't at the pool**, Ash copies a carving (chalk rubbing, a sketch: **Dexterity check DC 10** for a legible copy) and Zeke reads it at home that night. Moss is in the kitchen when he does.
- **C. Thistle recognizes the script** ("That's Old Mere-speech, the Warren Mothers' writing!"), and Zeke, looking over her shoulder, realizes he can read it too.

## 3. Scenes

**Route A:** the night before, Moss stops at the Fenwick door. He doesn't come in. *"Come with me tomorrow, lad. Before light. Bring your brothers."* He looks at Gideon, who is behind Ash in the hall, and says, more carefully: "If you'll come, Gideon." Gideon (R-003: a good neighbor, a strange man; he's never been easy around him) folds his arms, and then says, "I'll come." He doesn't say why. (He's coming to protect Ash from whatever Moss is. **Route C:** Thistle bangs on the Fenwick hatch at three in the morning. "He's gone to the gate without me again. Up. Up! I know the way. It smells like home."

Then the trees change: the orchards end, and the Fordwood begins, old oaks and older ash, moss on everything, and a quiet that feels less like an absence of sound and more like a held breath.*

### Scene 2 · The pool (★1)

**Getting there:** an hour's walk past the last orchard, off the road, down a deer path only Moss (and Thistle, by smell) can follow. **Without Moss or Thistle:** **Wisdom (Survival) DC 15**, or follow the stream upstream until it stops making any sound at all.

> *The trees open on a clearing nobody has cut. In the middle lies a pool, perfectly round, maybe forty feet across, and perfectly, unnaturally still: not a ripple, though the stream that should feed it is running. It doesn't reflect the sky quite right. The sky in it is a little greyer, and there are more stars.*
>
> *Around it stand seven stones, grey and furred with moss, some no higher than your chest, the tallest three times your height. Every one is carved, deep, in rows of marks like no letters you've seen. An enormous old ash tree leans out over the water from the far side, one long limb reaching almost to the middle.*
>
> *Moss walks into the shallows up to his knees, as if it's his own kitchen, and lays both hands flat on the water. And the water* pushes back*. You can see it: a slow bulge under his palms, as if something under the surface is leaning on a door.*

**What Moss does every morning (he tells them, now, plainly; `world/00` §5):**
- "This is the Ford Pool. It's a door. There are others, but this is the one under your city's feet, near enough."
- "It's been failing a long while. Someone's been… thinning it." (He means glimmer mining. He doesn't say so: "I'd rather not say until I'm sure who.")
- "Every morning I come and I hold it. That's all. I hold it." (He doesn't say he's been doing it for nineteen years. **Insight DC 15:** he's been doing it for a very long time. If Ash asks how long: "A while, lad.")
- "Thistle came through it. Something followed her." (The eel.)
- **What he won't say:** what he is; what Ash is; who's calling. "I'd rather not say."

**Ash at the pool (SEC-02, no roll):** the pool's stillness *hums*, very low, the way the Ford Stone did. Ash's locket is cold as ice. He can feel, if he stands near the water, the push from the other side, the way you can feel a crowd through a door.

**Thistle:** delighted and homesick and loud about both. She sticks her face nearly to the water and says "Hollowmere's just *there*." Moss tells her to step back. She doesn't.

**Gideon:** stands at the treeline with his hand on his sword and watches Moss the whole time. He touches the moss on the nearest stone, and goes still: it's the same warm green he saw on the Ford Stone. He says nothing. (SEC-09.)

**Zeke:** goes from stone to stone with his fingers on the carvings, muttering. "These aren't… it's not Old Eyrlandish. It's not Dwarvish. It's not Elvish. I *know* this. Why do I know this?" (★4 comes after the fight. Here he only half-reads. If he gets a minute with a stone now, **Intelligence (History) DC 15**: he reads one word, "Watcher," and loses it.)

**The day:** Moss means to hold the pool through the morning turn of light and go home. Instead the push doesn't stop. It *grows*. He stays. The party can stay with him through the day (the side chain below) or go and come back. **At sunset, Fight 2.**

**Through the day (optional quick checks, mild pressure):**
- **Zeke's rubbings:** **Dexterity DC 10** per stone, for a full set of copies. Pays off at ★4.
- **The shallows:** coins on the pool's edge under the water, hundreds of years of offerings. **Perception DC 12** to see that some are very old (square Varrow pennies, dead coin). See §7 (and Moss's view on taking them).
- **Scouting the clearing:** **Perception DC 13** or Ash from the top of the tallest stone (no check with climb speed): the treeline to the north has "gone quiet" since noon. No birds. Nothing. Bart: "Ash. The birds left. All of them. When birds leave, I leave. That's a rule."

### Scene 4 · Zeke reads the stones (★4)

After the fight, in the last grey light, Zeke kneels at the tallest stone. The fight did something to him (or the pool did). The carvings swim, and settle, and make sense.

> *Zeke's finger moves along a line of marks, slowly. His lips move. Then, out loud, uncertain, like a man reading a language from a dream:*
>
> *"Here… here the Ford-Watcher… and the Hart… sang the world in two."*
>
> *Behind you, Moss has gone the color of the stones.*
>
> *"Read it again," he says. "Ezekiel. Read it again."*

- **Moss never calls Zeke "Ezekiel."** Only Gideon does. Gideon notices.
- Zeke reads it again. Same words. He can read more, very slowly: the stones list names, and among them, four marks Zeke reads as **"the Hart, the Bear, the Heron, the Tortoise"** (SEC-08 seed, `campaign/03`). He doesn't know what they mean. (Neither do the brothers. Moss does.)
- **SEC-06 seed:** "sang the world in two" at *the Ford*. Moss, if asked what it means: "An old story. Older than the city." Then, quieter: "I'd rather not say, lad. Not yet."
- **SEC-01 seed #5:** Moss goes white at "the Hart." If anyone pushes now: **Insight DC 13** shows he's not angry, he's *shaken*. "I'll tell you. I swear I will. Not here."
- **Thistle**, whose ears have gone straight up: "The Hart. *The Stag.* That's in the songs. That's Old Mossb—" Moss looks at her. She stops. (R-069: she talks too much. This time she knows it.)
- **Zeke's discovery (`campaign/05` §6):** he can read Sundering-script. He doesn't know that's what it is. He will want to come back every day with a notebook. **R-006 (Zeke → Moss) moves via EVT-03:** Moss is no longer just "interesting." Zeke looks at him like a book he can't open.
- **Gideon:** says nothing at all. He's looking at the moss. There are tiny white flowers in it now, all round where Moss is standing. There weren't before.

### Scene 5 · Lark names herself

At the end of the fight, when the pool steadies (★3), or at the next turn of light if it didn't land:
> *The pool goes still: not the dead stillness of before, but the stillness of held breath let out. And up through it, clearer than you've ever heard her, as if she's in the next room:*
>
> *"That was you. I felt it. Everything went— quieter. The good kind of quiet." A sniff. "I'm Lark. Who are you?"*

- **She can hear words now, at the pool.** (The pool is a gate; the bond runs clearer through it. Elsewhere, still only humming; Ch5 brings words everywhere.) Whatever Ash says, she hears. Let the player say it. This is a big moment. Don't rush it.
- She'll ask his name, how old he is, whether he's real, whether he's a Tall Folk, whether he's coming. **Don't let her ask Ash to promise anything.** If Ash promises on his own, she believes him absolutely (R-064).
- **Thistle hears only Ash's half.** When Ash says "Lark," Thistle makes a sound like she's been hit. "*Lark?* Burdock's Lark? The little singer? That's who the Regent took!" She's crying, and furious, and hugging Ash, all at once. *(This is the first time the name and the voice connect. Moss closes his eyes.)*
- **Moss (R-014, the Mirrowen heir is the other half of the song, and Brannoc has her):** "Lark," he says, very quietly, as if he's filing it somewhere. Then, if pressed: "Then we know who's calling. That's enough for tonight."

### Scene 6 · Moss's upgrade (#1)

As the party catches its breath, something happens to Moss. Run it as a quiet image, not an announcement.

> *Moss sits down on a fallen trunk at the edge of the pool, the way an old man sits after a long day. Then he laughs. Just once, surprised. A thrush has landed on his knee. It cocks its head at him, and he cocks his head back, and says something to it, not words, a sound like wind going through leaves, and the thrush answers.*
>
> *Then Moss smiles. You've known him your whole life. You've never seen him smile like that, like he means all of it.*

- **Moss's story upgrade #1 (`campaign/05` §7):** the veil Moss held over Ash for 19 years has slipped off, because Ash's gift is awake and doesn't need hiding any more (Moss lets it go). **Moss can now speak with animals and plants at will (no spell slot)** and has **advantage on checks to sense Wellgates.** Note it on his sheet (play-kit note). Don't explain the mechanics to the player in story terms. Moss just says, "I feel lighter, lad. Like I put down something heavy."
- **SEC-01 seed #4:** the moss blooms with tiny white flowers wherever he stands. He speaks to the wolves' bodies in the wind-in-leaves language, gently, before he buries them under the moss (or before they're woken; §5).
- **If Ash asks what he put down:** "Something I'd been carrying for you, lad. You don't need it any more." He won't say more. That's true, and it's a lot.

## 5. Fight 2 · The Ford Pool

```
FIGHT 2 · The Ford Pool · Chapter 3 · Significant · Party level 3
Target numbers (rules/02 §3, L3): total HP 165 · enemy DPR 18 · AC 13–15 · attack +4–+5 · DC 12–13
Expected length: 6 rounds (5–7). Three phases; changes at rounds 3–4 and 6.
```

### HP math against the party

- **Party DPR at L3 (rules/02 §1):** Ash 14 · Gideon 8 · Zeke 7 · Moss 8 = **37**. Significant target 4.5 × 37 ≈ **165**.
- **Thunder vulnerability:** the Hushed are vulnerable to thunder, and Zeke and Moss both have *Thunderwave*. Unchecked, one good *Thunderwave* on four bunched wolves deals ~2d8 × 2 = 18 to each (60 HP of wolves gone in one round). That's why Phase 1's counter is **spread formation** (below), which holds Zeke's area spell to ~1.3 targets on average. Budget: +10 effective party DPR in Phase 1 from thunder, so Phase 1 is built at 60 HP for ~2.5 rounds at ~47 DPR minus misses and movement.
- **The pool on the field:**

| Phase | Enemies | HP |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 Hushed Wolves | 4 × 15 = 60 |
| 2 | Borra (Hushed bear-folk conscript) + 2 Hushed Wolves from the pool | 45 + 30 = 75 |
| 3 | 2 Greywilt Tendrils (grasping from the tear) | 2 × 15 = 30 |
| **Total** | | **165** ✓ |

- **Waking Borra** (DC 15 Performance) removes his 45 HP from the fight without damage. That's the hope lever, and it costs Ash an action he also needs for the gate. Good. Expect 5 rounds if Ash wakes Borra early, 6–7 if not.

### Enemy DPR against the party

- **Party AC:** Gideon 18, Moss 16 (beast form 13 + 4 = 17), Ash 15, Zeke 14 (Mage Armor; the Harness, his Arcane Ward, absorbs the first ~10).
- **Phase 1:** 4 wolves × Bite +4 vs AC ~15.5 (47.5%, rising to ~72% with Pack Tactics, which they'll usually have) × 7 = **~4.2 each, ~17 total**, plus prone riders. ✓ (target 18)
- **Phase 2:** Borra 2 × Spear +5 vs AC ~16 (50%) × 7.5 = 7.5 · 2 fresh wolves ~8.4 · surviving Phase 1 wolves (usually 0–1) ~4 · greywilt Calm (no damage; costs reactions) → **~16–20.** ✓
- **Phase 3:** 2 tendrils × Grasp +5 (50%) × 5.5 = 5.5 · any survivors · the Tear's actions (one per round, ~5 average) → **~12–18**, with the objective doing the real work. ✓
- **Spike:** the Tear's stage-3 surge (§ stat block) on the round Phase 3 starts: ~22 across several targets. That's the phase-change spike.

### Battlefield

The Ford Pool clearing. (Crop-friendly map: a circle of pool in the middle, seven stones around it, the ash tree on the east side, the treeline to the north, the stream entering from the south.)

1. **The pool.** 40 ft across, perfectly still. The **shallows** (a 5-ft ring at the edge) are knee-deep: difficult terrain. Beyond them it's deep, and *wrong*: a creature that goes fully under at any point during this fight makes a **DC 12 Constitution save** or gains a level of Exhaustion (a taste of the drowning-dream; `world/00` §3). It doesn't cross: it isn't the turn of light *and* wanting to. (After sunset's minute passes, it's just very cold, very deep water.)
2. **The seven standing stones.** 8 to 25 ft tall, mossy, carved. **Three-quarters cover** behind one. Ash climbs them at his speed (Second-Story Work). From the top of the tallest (25 ft), he sees the whole clearing and can drop onto an enemy (**jump down 10+ ft onto a creature: as part of an attack, if he hits, +1d6 bludgeoning and the target makes a DC 13 Strength save or is knocked prone**; Ash takes no falling damage if the attack hits, and normal falling damage if it misses).
3. **The ash tree.** On the east bank, leaning out over the water, its long limb reaching to 10 ft from the pool's centre, 15 ft up. Ash can run along it (Acrobatics DC 10, or no check with climb speed if he goes on all fours). From its end he's **within 10 ft of the centre of the pool**: the best place to hum from (advantage on Hold the Gate hums, because the water can *hear* him there). Also the most exposed place in the clearing.
4. **The fallen trunk bridge.** Across the stream to the south: the only dry crossing. A 5-ft-wide choke point. Gideon on it is a wall.
5. **The treeline (north).** Dense oak and holly. Wolves come out of it in Phase 1. Heavily obscured beyond 10 ft in.
6. **Greywilt (from Phase 2).** See the Tear.

### Opening

- **Sunset.** The party is at the pool with Moss (or arriving, Route B).
- **Where everyone is:** Moss in the shallows on the west side, hands on the water. The rest wherever the player put them during the day. Thistle on a stone with her sling.
- **What the Hushed know:** nothing. They aren't hunting the party. They're being *pushed* through by the tear, and they go for whatever's warm and moving and loud. They're obedient to the stillness, and the stillness wants Moss's hands off the water.
- **Telegraph (the round before the fight):** the pool shivers once, all over, like a horse's skin. A thin grey film, like frost on a window, creeps up the north side of the nearest stone. Every bird in the clearing goes silent at once. Ash's locket burns cold.

### Enemy plan

- **Phase 1 focus: Moss**, because he's holding the door. Two wolves go straight for him in the shallows; two flank whoever is weakest-looking and nearest (usually Zeke). They keep apart from each other.
- **If Moss is guarded too well:** they switch to the softest target (Zeke, then Ash) and try to knock people prone into the shallows.
- **Borra's focus (Phase 2):** whoever is hurting the wolves most (usually Gideon or Moss's beast form). He's a soldier; he fights like one.
- **Flee or surrender:** never. They're Hushed.

### PHASE 1 · rounds 1–3 · "Out of the trees"

- **Four Hushed Wolves** come out of the treeline at a lope, silent: no growls, no panting, grey eyes like wet slate. Initiative −2 (they're slow to react, then fast).
- **Counter (rules/02 §6), telegraphed:** **spread formation.** Describe it as they come: *"They don't bunch like dogs. They spread, ten or fifteen feet apart, like they've been told to."* They never end a turn within 10 ft of another wolf unless they're both on the same target. Zeke's *Thunderwave* will usually catch one, sometimes two.
- **Objective:** keep the wolves off Moss. Moss can't Wild Shape and hold the gate at the same time. In Phase 1 he can fight (he hasn't started straining yet): his choice each round (DM: he holds every other round; on the others he fights).
- **Telegraph for Phase 2 (end of round 2 or 3):** the pool bulges in the middle, a slow dome of water rising, and the grey film on the stones *spreads*, racing up them like frost on glass. Moss gasps: "It's tearing. Lad, it's tearing." He goes to both knees in the shallows.

### PHASE 2 · trigger: start of round 3 or 4 (whichever comes after the telegraph) · **TERRAIN change**

- **The tear opens** (stage 1, see the Tear block). From now on, Moss must **Hold** every round (his action), or the tear advances. He can't attack, cast or change shape while holding. (He can still concentrate on a spell he cast earlier.)
- **Greywilt spreads 5 ft per round** outward from the pool's edge across the clearing, starting at the shallows. Greywilt ground is **difficult terrain**, and any creature that **starts its turn** on it makes a **DC 12 Wisdom saving throw** or is **Calmed** until the start of its next turn: it can't take reactions, and it has disadvantage on attack rolls against Hushed creatures (a terrible gentle calm; it doesn't *want* to). Moss's beast forms and Gideon have advantage on this save (old green things, old oaths). Hushed creatures ignore greywilt.
- **Borra rises.** A bear-folk soldier, eight feet tall, in sodden scale and a Horned Host tabard with an iron-horn badge, walks up out of the middle of the pool as if climbing stairs, water pouring off him, grey from his claws to his shoulders. His muzzle and eyes are still brown. He carries a spear and a round shield. **Two more Hushed Wolves** come up behind him.
- **Ash can tell (no roll, and only Ash):** Borra is **"still in there."** Grey to the shoulders, not all the way. Under the silence, very faintly, a heartbeat that's frightened. *(This is Ash's first sense of who can be woken. Ch5 makes it a discovery moment. Here it's a hint.)*
- **Counter (Phase 2), telegraphed:** **Borra's Shield-Shove.** The round he rises, he looks straight at Gideon, sets his shield, and lowers his head. Next round he shoves Gideon 10 ft away from whoever Gideon is protecting (Strength save DC 13), so the wolves can get at the softer target. Answers Gideon's Protection style. Once per round, and only Gideon.
- **Gideon's oath, if Ch2's vigil was skipped (Ch2 ★5 Route C):** the first time Gideon steps between a Hushed creature and Ash, the moss on the nearest standing stone glows warm green, lifts off like mist, and settles on him. Gideon feels a great bear, gone, and something antlered, very near (and this time the antlered thing is *right there* in the shallows; he looks at Moss and looks away). **Gideon is level 3 from this moment** (Oath of the Ancients). Everyone else levels at the end of the chapter; the L2 roster adjustments below still apply.

### PHASE 3 · trigger: round 6, or the tear reaching stage 2 · **OBJECTIVE change** (hold the gate)

- **The tear widens to stage 2** (or is already there). Moss, holding, is visibly failing: his nose bleeds, his hands shake, the water's dome pushes up between his palms. He says, through his teeth: *"I can't hold it alone. I can't."*
- **Two Greywilt Tendrils** reach up out of the pool's edge like grey glass vines. They grab at whoever is nearest the water, especially Moss and Ash, and try to pull them in.
- **★3 lands here.** Run **Hold the Gate** (§4). Prompt Ash with sensation, never instruction: *"The tune is in your throat. The water is waiting."* If Ash is down or prone at the water (Route B), the hum comes out of him on its own, half-dreaming. If Thistle's conscious, she shouts Route C's line at the moment it fits.
- **The Tear's spike:** the round Phase 3 starts, the Tear makes its **Stage Surge** (see the block). That's the phase-change spike.
- **Telegraph for the end:** each successful hum makes the clearing's sound come back a little: a cricket, the stream, a single bird. The greywilt stops spreading on the first success and starts to *recede* on the second.

### FINAL BEAT · how it ends

- **The gate holds** (3 Hold the Gate successes). The tear shrinks to a hairline. The greywilt on the stones dries and flakes off like old paint. The tendrils sink. **Any Hushed creature still on the field stops** where it stands, as if it's forgotten why it came: it stands still, grey and empty, and doesn't attack unless attacked. Each one can now be put down (mercifully, no attack roll needed) or woken.
- **Waking (the hope lever):** Ash, within 30 ft, hums the tune at a Hushed creature as an action: **Charisma (Performance) DC 15** (L1–4 tier). Bart, Thistle or Zeke can Help. **Borra** can be woken during the fight (it takes him out of it at once) or after. **The wolves** can be woken too, and if they are, they shake, and whine, and run off into the Fordwood: Moss watches them go with an expression the party won't see on him again for a long time.
  - **Act I waking ruling (see `_index.md` and `_change-requests.md`):** in Act I, Ash can only wake creatures that are **half-Hushed** (grey to the elbows or shoulders, still "in there," like Borra and these wolves, who were hushed in the last season). He can't wake the **fully Hushed** (gone wholly grey, or gone under still water at a turn of light) until Ch12's duet or Ch15's discovery. Ash can *feel* the difference, which is how the player learns the rule.
- **If Borra is killed:** he falls back into the pool and sinks without a sound. The grey slides off him in the water, and for a moment he's a frightened young bear-folk, and then he's gone. Moss doesn't say anything. He doesn't have to. **No Mercy point.**

### Fail-forward

- **The tear reaches stage 4** (or the party is losing badly): Moss stands up in the shallows, and something happens that nobody sees clearly. The light goes green.
